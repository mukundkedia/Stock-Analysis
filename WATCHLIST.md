## 1. MAS Financial Services Ltd (NSE: MASFIN · BSE: 540749)

- **Status:** 🟢 BUY / ADD — then 🟡 re-evaluate after Q3 FY27 results (Oct–Dec 2026 quarter, due ~late Jan 2027).
- **Sector:** NBFC — MSME / micro-enterprise lending (Ahmedabad; Gujarat-heavy).
- **Price when reviewed:** ₹265.46 (6-Nov-2026) · Market cap ~₹4,817 cr
- **Valuation:** P/E ~11.5× · P/B ~1.5× (book value ₹178) · Dividend yield ~0.75%
- **Growth score (portfolio review):** 8 / 10 — highest in the portfolio.
- **Current holding:** Asha Kedia — 60 shares (20 bought Sep-2023 @ ₹891 + 40 bonus from 2:1 bonus in Feb-2024); avg cost ~₹297; ~−11% on paper.
- **Analyse with:** `.claude/skills/nbfc-analysis` framework.

### What the business does
Borrows from 40+ banks/institutions (~9.2–9.4% cost) and lends to small
businesses and individuals: micro-enterprise loans (largest), SME loans,
two-wheeler & used commercial-vehicle loans, and housing loans via subsidiary
MAS Rural Housing. Distinctive **partner model** — lends a large share through
smaller NBFCs/MFIs who on-lend to rural/small-town borrowers — plus direct
branches and sale/co-lending of loans with banks (off-book up to ~25% of AUM).

### Positives we discussed
1. **Consistent growth:** profit up in 13 consecutive quarters; revenue +21–29% YoY every quarter. 10-yr profit CAGR 21%, 5-yr 21%, 3-yr 23%.
2. **Latest quarter (Q2 FY27):** AUM ₹17,043 cr (+23% YoY); net profit ₹115 cr (+26%); management guides 20–25% AUM growth.
3. **Steady ROA ~2.9%** for six years (FY21–FY26) through COVID, rate hikes and MFI stress — disciplined lending. Target ~3%.
4. **Conservative leverage:** borrowings ÷ equity ~3.7× (Sep-2026) — lower than many peers.
5. **Strong liquidity:** ~₹990 cr cash + ₹929 cr investments (Sep-2026); LCR 342% (Mar-2026, RBI min 100%); positive ALM gaps in every bucket up to 5 years.
6. **Diversified funding:** term loans 62.7%, NCD/CP 22.6%, CC/OD 14.3% (Mar-2026); 40+ lenders incl. SBI, SIDBI, BoB, Union Bank, Canara, PNB, HDFC Bank, IndusInd; CARE **AA- (Stable)**, CP **A1+** (Jun-2026).
7. **Collateral:** most loans backed by property mortgage or vehicle hypothecation; partner loans backed by receivables. (Last disclosed unsecured share ~22% in 2017 — needs update.)
8. **Cheap vs history and peers:** P/E 11.5× vs 30× (2021) and 16–20× (FY23–26); at par with Five-Star (11.2×), cheaper than SBFC (20×), Home First (18.5×), Chola (24.5×).
9. **Institutional interest rising:** DII holding 8% (Dec-2023) → 20% (Sep-2026). Promoter holding fell only due to the 2024 QIP, not selling.
10. **Analyst view:** InCred Equities "Add", target ₹400 (2026).

### Risks / why the stock has been flat
- **Derating:** EPS +161% (Mar-2021 → now) but P/E 30× → 11.5×, so price flat. Causes: rich starting valuation, ROE down from ~18% (FY18–20) to ~13.5%, 10% dilution from 2024 QIP, sector-wide NBFC/MSME derating, FII selling in 2026 (stock −28% Mar → Sep 2026; no company-specific bad news found).
- **ROE ~13.5%** ≈ cost of equity → no valuation premium until it rises.
- **Rising cost of funds:** 9.2% → guided 9.4–9.5% by FY27 exit (margin pressure).
- **Credit-cost proxy rising:** Screener "Other expenses" 15% of revenue (FY24) → ~24% (Q2 FY27).
- **Asset quality:** Gross Stage-3 guided 2.5–2.75% — higher than housing peers; MSME borrowers vulnerable in a slowdown.
- **Concentration:** Gujarat-heavy; partner-NBFC model means a partner failure can sour a whole pool.
- **Growth > ROE:** ~23% growth vs ~13.5% ROE ⇒ likely another equity raise in a few years.
- Small-cap liquidity; falling retail shareholder count (51k → 39k).

### Re-evaluation checklist (after each quarterly result)
| Check | Last value | Keep thesis if |
|---|---|---|
| AUM growth YoY | +23% (Q2 FY27) | ≥ 20% |

| Rating | CARE AA- Stable | no downgrade / negative outlook |
| P/E · P/B | 11.5× · 1.5× | re-check vs growth |

**Exit triggers:** Gross Stage-3 > 3.5%, ROA < 2.5% for 2 quarters, rating
downgrade, AUM growth < 15%, or management cutting growth guidance.

## 2. Aavas Financiers Ltd (NSE: AAVAS · BSE: 541988)

- **Status:** 🟢 BUY / 🟡 REVIEW — buy at the current low valuation; review whether growth picks up under CVC after Q3 FY27 results (Oct–Dec 2026 quarter, due ~late Jan 2027).
- **Sector:** Housing finance company (NBFC-HFC) — affordable home loans to low/middle-income, mostly self-employed borrowers in semi-urban and rural India (Jaipur HQ; Rajasthan-led; 443 branches in 15 states).
- **Price when reviewed:** ₹1,137.7 (6-Nov-2026) · Market cap ~₹9,021 cr · 52-week high/low ₹1,775 / ₹1,050
- **Valuation:** P/E ~12.8× · P/B ~1.7× (book value ₹681) · No dividend (all profit reinvested)
- **Growth score (portfolio review):** 6 / 10 (was HOLD; upgraded to Buy/Review on 7-Nov-2026 at user's request).
- **Current holding:** Kailash Kumar Kedia — 8 shares bought Nov-2023 @ ₹1,500.23; ~−24% on paper.
- **Analyse with:** `.claude/skills/nbfc-analysis` framework.
- **Note:** A YouTube short on Aavas (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LlYRsrUVRDM) was referenced but not reviewed (YouTube blocked in session) — summarise its claims and check them against this entry.

### What the business does
Gives home loans (purchase, construction, extension, repair) and loans against
property to customers banks find hard to serve — shopkeepers, traders,
contractors, dairy farmers and low-income salaried people without formal income
proof. Staff visit the home/business to assess real cash flows and check the
property title (its core skill). Typical ticket roughly ₹10–15 lakh (estimate).
Almost every loan is **secured by a mortgage on the house/property**.
Funded by bank term loans, National Housing Bank refinance, NCDs and loan sales
to banks. Controlled by **CVC Capital Partners** (48.9%) since 2024–25.

### Positives we discussed
1. **Cleanest asset quality among the NBFCs discussed:** Gross NPA 1.11%, Net NPA 0.70%, credit cost 0.24% (guided ~0.25%) — Q2 FY27.
2. **High, steady ROA ~3.3%** (FY26, computed from Screener) — higher than MAS (~2.9%).
3. **Strong margin:** NIM ~7.8%, up 11 bp QoQ; financing margin steady at 32–37% of revenue (vs MAS 26%).
4. **Conservative leverage:** borrowings ₹16,485 cr ÷ equity ₹5,398 cr ≈ **3.1×** (Sep-2026) — low for an HFC (peers often 5–7×), leaves room to grow.
5. **Steady growth:** AUM ~₹24,800 cr (+16% YoY); disbursements +21% (H1 up to +30%); profit 5-yr CAGR 18%; Q2 FY27 PAT ₹182 cr (+10.7%).
6. **Cheapest valuation in its history:** P/E 12.8× and P/B 1.7× vs ~60× and 7–8× in 2021; about 36% below CVC's ₹1,767 open-offer price.
7. **Management guidance:** FY27 AUM growth 16–17%, aspiration 20%+ under CVC; spreads expected to hold above 5%.
8. **Domestic institutions buying:** DII holding 10.8% → 22.4% (Mar-2025 → Sep-2026).

### Risks / why the stock has fallen
- **Derating:** profit +18%/yr over 5 years but share price −16%/yr (≈₹2,800 in 2021 → ₹1,138); −28% in the last year. Premium valuation of 2021 unwound as growth slowed and ROE stayed ~14%.
- **Growth slowing:** profit growth down to ~11% (Q2 FY27) from 14–18%; AUM growth stuck in mid-teens despite 20%+ aspiration.
- **ROE ~13.9%** ≈ cost of equity → no premium until growth/ROE improve (note: low ROE here is due to low leverage, not weak lending — ROA is high).
- **Private-equity overhang:** CVC will eventually sell; stock is well below its ₹1,767 entry.
- **FII selling:** FII holding 29.7% → 15.2% in a year.
- **Competition** from banks and other HFCs in affordable housing; opex-heavy branch model.
- **Concentration:** Rajasthan is the largest state.

### Re-evaluation checklist (after each quarterly result)
| Check | Last value | Upgrade to firm BUY / ADD if | Downgrade if |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUM growth YoY | +16% (Q2 FY27) | ≥ 18–20% | < 14% |
| Disbursement growth YoY | +21% | stays ≥ 20% | turns negative |
| Profit growth YoY | +10.7% | ≥ 15%, ≈ AUM growth | < 10% for 2 quarters |
| ROA | ~3.3% | ≥ 3.2% | < 3.0% |
| Gross / Net NPA | 1.11% / 0.70% | stable | GNPA > 1.5% |
| 1+ DPD (early overdue) | 3.81% | falling | rising for 2 quarters |
| Credit cost | 0.24% | ≤ 0.3% | > 0.5% |
| NIM | ~7.8% | stable/rising | falls below 7% |
| Leverage (borrowings ÷ equity) | ~3.1× | rising towards 4× with steady ROA | — |
| CVC stake | 48.9% | stable | large block sale / exit announced |
| P/E · P/B | 12.8× · 1.7× | re-check vs growth | — |

**Exit triggers:** GNPA above ~2%, AUM growth below 12% for 2 quarters, ROA below 2.8%, or a rating downgrade.

- **Status:** 🟠 TRIM 50% — sell half, keep half for the dividend; move back to HOLD if growth returns (see triggers).
- **Sector:** Government-owned NBFC — Infrastructure Finance Company (NBFC-IFC), power-sector lender; subsidiary of PFC (52.63%); Maharatna; Public Financial Institution (issues 54EC bonds).
- **Price when reviewed:** ₹297 (Screener, 8-Nov-2026) · Market cap ~₹78,180 cr · 52-week ₹388 / ₹282

- **Analyse with:** `.claude/skills/govt-infra-lenders` (plus `nbfc-analysis` basics).

### What the business does
Borrows cheaply (bonds incl. 54EC capital-gain bonds, foreign loans, bank loans) and lends to state power utilities (distribution, generation, transmission), central power PSUs, private and renewable power projects, and increasingly non-power infrastructure. Loan book ₹6.17 lakh cr (Sep-2026).

### Positives
- **High ROA for a wholesale lender:** ~2.6% (FY26, computed) thanks to tiny operating costs.
- **Clean asset quality:** GNPA 0.88% / NNPA 0.20% (Dec-2025, latest found); Stage-2 loans down 80% YoY to ₹3,235 cr (Q2 FY27); most loans backed by state guarantees.
- **Volumes recovering:** Q2 FY27 disbursements ₹53,541 cr (H1 ₹79,207 cr); loan book +5% QoQ; FY27 disbursement target ~₹2 lakh cr; sanctioned pipeline ~₹2.56 lakh cr (₹65–70k cr near disbursement — broker note).
- **Diversifying:** renewables book ~₹75k cr (+30%), transmission, infra & logistics.
- **Very cheap with high income:** 5× earnings, below book, ~6% dividend yield; net worth ₹94,325 cr (+14%).

### Risks
- **Profit falling:** down YoY three quarters running (−22%, −6%, −21%); Q2 FY27 PAT ₹3,478 cr (−21%); H1 −15%.
- **Margin squeeze:** NIM 3.43% (FY26) → 3.22% (H1 FY27); higher funding costs; Motilal Oswal called Q2 an earnings miss.
- **Weak FY26 growth:** loan book ~+3% as state discoms prepaid / refinanced with banks.
- **Guidance only moderate:** ~10–12% loan growth (one report says cut to 10–11%).
- **Provision coverage fell** ~72% → ~51%.
- **Concentration** on financially weak state utilities; leverage ~7.7× assets/equity (ROE ~20% is mostly leverage); government ownership limits re-rating; FX/hedging losses.
